Statement of GDPR alignment

Statement of GDPR alignment

This page provides a supplemental compliance notice for individuals located in the European Economic Area (EEA), the United Kingdom, Switzerland, or other jurisdictions that expect GDPR-style transparency in relation to data handling.

It applies to:

  • website visitors
  • prospective clients (leads)
  • clients engaging in consultation or service stages

The purpose of this notice is interpretive. It explains how the data-handling framework of Immigrate to Brazil aligns with GDPR principles, particularly in the context of:

  • digital intake systems
  • cross-border advisory services
  • remote communication and coordination

This notice should be read together with the Privacy Policy, which provides the broader and general framework for data processing.

This page does not expand processing beyond what is described in the Privacy Policy. It clarifies it through a GDPR-oriented lens.

Section 02

Data controller identification

For the purposes of the processing activities described in this notice, the data controller is:

Immigrate to Brazil immigratetobrazilteam@gmail.com

This includes processing related to:

  • website interaction
  • consultation requests
  • intake forms and communications
  • advisory and coordination services

The role of the data controller is to determine the purposes and means of processing personal data within the scope described.

Where legal services are provided through a qualified lawyer, the lawyer may act as an independent data controller for processing carried out within the scope of legal representation. In such cases, the lawyer-client agreement governs that processing relationship.

Section 03

Legal basis for processing

Personal data is processed only where a valid legal basis exists under applicable data protection frameworks.

Depending on the context, processing may rely on:

  • pre-contractual necessity (e.g. responding to inquiries, assessing eligibility, preparing consultations)
  • contractual necessity (e.g. delivering agreed advisory or coordination services)
  • legitimate interests (e.g. maintaining operational integrity, improving service structure, preventing misuse)
  • legal obligations (e.g. compliance with regulatory or record-keeping requirements)
  • consent, where appropriate (e.g. optional tracking technologies or non-essential communications)

Legal basis is determined based on function, not generalized wording. Each processing activity is aligned with its appropriate justification.

Section 04

Categories of data processed (contextual clarification)

Depending on the interaction stage, data processed may include:

  • identification and contact details
  • nationality and residency-related information
  • immigration-related records or documentation voluntarily provided
  • communication records
  • technical and usage data related to website interaction

Processing is limited to what is necessary for the defined purpose at each stage. Data is not collected or retained beyond what is proportionate to the service provided.

Section 05

Explicit EU client rights

Where GDPR or equivalent frameworks apply, individuals may have the following rights, subject to applicable conditions:

  • right of access - to obtain confirmation and a copy of personal data
  • right to rectification - to correct inaccurate or incomplete data
  • right to erasure - where applicable and not restricted by legal obligations
  • right to restriction of processing - in defined circumstances
  • right to data portability - where technically feasible and applicable
  • right to object - where processing is based on legitimate interests
  • right to withdraw consent - where consent is the legal basis

These rights are not absolute and may be limited where necessary to comply with legal obligations, professional duties, or legitimate operational requirements.

Section 06

Cross-border transfer acknowledgment

Immigrate to Brazil operates in a cross-border context and serves an international client base.

As part of normal operations, personal data may be accessed, processed, or stored in jurisdictions outside the EEA, UK, or Switzerland where necessary for:

  • service delivery
  • communication
  • infrastructure and hosting
  • operational coordination

Where such transfers occur, safeguards are applied in a manner proportionate to the operational context. These may include:

  • use of reputable service providers
  • access controls and internal handling standards
  • contractual or practical measures aligned with data protection principles

Cross-border processing is inherent to international immigration services and is handled with attention to proportionality and necessity.

Section 07

Data retention and minimization principles

Personal data is retained only for as long as necessary to:

  • fulfill the purpose for which it was collected
  • comply with legal or regulatory obligations
  • maintain appropriate records of services provided

Retention periods are not fixed universally. They depend on the nature of the service, the stage reached, and applicable legal requirements.

Data that is no longer necessary is deleted or securely handled in accordance with internal practices.

Section 08

Relationship with legal representation and governing contracts

Where a client engages a qualified lawyer, the lawyer may act as an independent data controller for processing carried out within the scope of legal services.

In such cases:

  • the lawyer-client agreement governs the processing of personal data within that relationship
  • professional legal obligations may apply to data handling and retention
  • any inconsistency between this notice and the lawyer-client agreement is resolved in favor of the lawyer-client agreement with respect to legal services

Immigrate to Brazil does not override or modify the legal obligations of licensed professionals.

Section 09

Complaint process

If an individual believes that their data protection rights have been infringed, and applicable law provides the right to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority, they may do so.

For individuals in the EEA, UK, or Switzerland, this may include contacting the relevant national data protection authority.

Direct contact with Immigrate to Brazil is encouraged in the first instance, as many issues can be resolved efficiently at an operational level. However, this does not replace or limit the right to escalate a complaint to a supervisory authority.
