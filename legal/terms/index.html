Nature of service

Nature of service

Immigrate to Brazil operates as an immigration advisory and execution-support provider. Our role is to structure, guide, coordinate, and support immigration-related processes, including residency, visas, nationality pathways, regularization, and related Brazil-entry or Brazil-stay matters.

Our services are procedural and advisory in nature. They are designed to improve clarity, reduce error, and organize the execution of immigration processes, but they do not replace the authority of governmental or judicial bodies.

Immigrate to Brazil is not a government entity. We do not issue visas, grant residency, approve nationality, or control the outcome of any administrative or legal process. All such decisions remain exclusively within the jurisdiction of the relevant authorities.

Where legal representation is required or provided, this is performed by a qualified lawyer under a separate and specific legal engagement. That legal relationship is governed by its own contract and professional framework.

Section 02

Service scope definition

All services provided by Immigrate to Brazil are scope-based and stage-defined.

Public platform content is informational in nature and does not constitute individualized advice. Initial contact or general communication does not create an obligation to provide services beyond the defined scope.

Consultation services represent a structured advisory stage with defined objectives and deliverables. Execution support, where accepted, is a separate stage involving planning, coordination, and procedural support.

No assumption should be made that:

  • a prior interaction establishes a full-service relationship
  • a payment for one stage extends to subsequent stages
  • a consultation includes execution or representation

Each stage must be explicitly defined, accepted, and, where applicable, formalized through appropriate documentation.

Where legal services are engaged, the applicable lawyer-client agreement defines the precise scope of legal responsibility.

Section 03

Process-based engagement model

Immigrate to Brazil operates through a structured, phase-based model designed to manage complexity and reduce procedural risk.

The standard framework includes:

  • route fit and intake control
  • chronology and record review
  • document quality and execution planning
  • authority-facing preparation, filing support, and follow-through

This sequence is not interchangeable. Each phase builds on the integrity of the previous one.

Immigration processes are highly sensitive to:

  • timing
  • documentation accuracy
  • sequencing of actions
  • consistency of historical records

For this reason, progression between phases depends on proper completion of prior stages and may be paused, adjusted, or declined where foundational elements are incomplete or inconsistent.

Where legal representation becomes necessary, the transition to lawyer-led work is governed by a separate engagement contract.

Section 04

Client obligations

Clients are responsible for providing accurate, complete, and timely information at all stages of the process.

This includes, but is not limited to:

  • identity and civil status details
  • full and accurate chronology of events
  • prior filings, refusals, or communications with authorities
  • relevant documentation and supporting records
  • deadlines and time-sensitive developments

Clients must also:

  • respond to requests for information within a reasonable timeframe
  • provide documents in usable and accurate form
  • confirm instructions and approvals when required
  • maintain active communication during ongoing service stages

Failure to meet these obligations may affect:

  • the quality of advisory output
  • the viability of a proposed route
  • the timing and execution of a process

Immigrate to Brazil is not responsible for consequences arising from incomplete, inaccurate, or delayed client-provided information.

Section 05

No guarantee clause

Immigrate to Brazil does not guarantee any specific outcome, including but not limited to:

  • visa approval
  • residency grant or renewal
  • nationality recognition
  • status regularization
  • administrative or judicial decisions

All outcomes are determined by government authorities, courts, or other competent bodies.

Additionally, a route that appears viable at an initial or general level may change after:

  • detailed chronology review
  • document verification
  • authority-specific requirements
  • regulatory or policy changes

The role of Immigrate to Brazil is to structure and support the process, not to control the outcome.
