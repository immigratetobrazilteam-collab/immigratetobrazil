Intake purpose

Intake purpose

Our forms are designed to support intake control. They help us identify who is contacting us, what issue is being presented, what route may be relevant, what chronology is already known, and what type of review is appropriate as the next step.

Structured intake reduces confusion and reduces the cost of weak first contact. In practical terms, that means fewer avoidable follow-up gaps and a better first-stage review.

Section 02

Information requested

Our intake systems may request full name, nationality or country context, contact details, occupation, immigration stage, service category, optional reference details, document uploads, and a written summary of the matter.

These fields exist because route fit, chronology, document planning, and communication control depend on them. They are not included as unnecessary volume.

Section 03

Document submission

Where upload is enabled, users may submit records relevant to the matter. That may include authority notices, identity records, prior filings, translations, payment proof, relationship records, or other documents necessary to understand procedural posture.

Uploads should be disciplined. The objective is relevance, not bulk.

Section 04

Sensitive and excessive information

Do not submit more sensitive material than the current stage reasonably requires. If you are unsure whether a record should be sent, begin with a controlled summary or ask for guidance first.

Data minimization is part of intake quality. Sending everything at once is not always the safest or most efficient approach.

Section 05

Manual review

All submissions are reviewed manually. Submission does not trigger automated analysis, automatic legal evaluation, or immediate consultation booking. It creates a structured record for controlled review.

The next step may be clarification, consultation, payment instruction, execution-stage discussion, or direction toward a different route if the current presentation suggests the wrong category.

Section 06

No representation by submission alone

Form submission does not create representation, open-ended advisory responsibility, or guaranteed service commitment. Representation begins only after defined scope is accepted in writing.

That distinction protects both the client and the process.

Section 07

Accuracy obligation

The submitting party is responsible for the accuracy and completeness of the information provided. Missing deadlines, omitted filings, inaccurate chronology, or incomplete relationship facts can materially affect route assessment and execution planning.

If material facts change after submission, those changes should be communicated rather than assumed to be irrelevant.

Section 08

Technical issues

If a form fails, an upload appears incomplete, or a technical issue prevents proper submission, contact immigratetobrazilteam@gmail.com. If the matter is urgent, note that urgency separately and clearly.

Duplicate submissions should be avoided unless the first attempt clearly failed.
