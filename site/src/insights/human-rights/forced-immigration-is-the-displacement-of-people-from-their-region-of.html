Overview

Forced immigration is the displacement of people from their region of origin due to circumstances beyond their control, such as war, persecution, armed conflict, violence, human rights violations or natural disasters. Climate refugees are a subset of this phenomenon and refer to people who are forced to leave their homes due to extreme weather events such as hurricanes, floods, prolonged droughts or rising sea levels. These climate changes can destroy livelihoods, make habitable areas unsafe and lead to shortages of basic resources, forcing people to seek refuge elsewhere. The increase in extreme weather conditions due to climate change linked to pollution and global warming is contributing to an increase in the number of climate refugees around the world, posing significant challenges for the international community in terms of protecting and assisting these vulnerable populations.
