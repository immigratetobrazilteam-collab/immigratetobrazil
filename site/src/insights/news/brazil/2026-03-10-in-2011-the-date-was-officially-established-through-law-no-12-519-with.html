Overview

In 2011, the date was officially established through Law No. 12,519, with the aim of marking the end of slavery in Brazil. This date honors the day of the death of Zumbi dos Palmares, leader of Quilombo de Palmares, who dedicated his life to preserving the way of life of enslaved Africans who managed to escape oppression. The day promotes a series of initiatives to combat racism and revive discussions about the arrival of Africans in the country, the period of slavery in Brazil and the persistence of structural racism in our society. The opportunity for spaces for black voices begins with your list of authors of books and podcasts that you consume on a daily basis. In addition to seeking to recognize the importance of diversity, actively supporting racial equality in all spheres of life to develop a fairer and more inclusive society for all. Fighting racism is also about doing justice.
